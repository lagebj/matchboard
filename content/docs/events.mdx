## What

Events cover cups, tournaments, and friendly days — occasions that need their own temporary
squads, built from your player pool, without touching league round selections.

## Why

A cup day squad is a different kind of decision than a normal league round: it usually needs a
one-off, event-specific balance of players rather than the season-long team structure your
league rounds already use. Keeping events separate means a tournament squad never creates a
league selection, never affects league fairness history, and never touches a finalized match.

## How

Create an event, mark player availability for it, and generate one or more squads: a single
competitive squad built around your strongest available line-up, a balanced split across
several squads, or squads seeded manually and balanced automatically around your existing
assignments. A player you assign to a squad yourself is respected automatically — there is no
separate "lock" step for an individual assignment. Two operations solve different problems on
top of that: **Fill remaining places** only adds currently-unassigned eligible players to squads
below their target size, and never moves anyone already assigned; **Regenerate automatic plan**
preserves your manual assignments and recalculates the rest. When the whole squad list is
settled, lock it as a set — that still runs a validation pass and protects it from further
automatic changes.

Matches within an event can share helpers across squads when their matches do not overlap in
time, similar to League helpers in normal rounds. Set the number of halves, the duration of each
half, and the break between halves on the event — most events use a single continuous period, but
a cup or fixture played as a first and second half can be configured with 2 halves. Overlap checks
for helpers use the full match length (halves × half duration, plus any break between halves).

A squad can also override the event's game format, formation, halves, half duration, and break
duration individually — useful when one event mixes formats, for example a 7v7 squad playing
2×17 minutes with a 1 minute break alongside a 9v9 squad playing 2×20 minutes with a 1 minute
break on the same event day. Target squad size is always set per squad, not shared across the
event.

## Example

For the Fjord Cup, Fjordvik FK enters two squads. The coach generates one competitive squad
built around the strongest available line-up and lets the rest of the available players fill a
second, balanced squad automatically.

## What Matchboard does next

Event squads and matches get their own post-match reporting, separate from league statistics —
an event appearance is real playing time for the player, but it is not counted as a league
match.
