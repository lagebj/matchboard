## What

The Players page has four modes: an overview of actual participation and match statistics,
current-round attention for a selected round, a development view of active coaching focus per
player, and base group management for stable team assignment.

## Why

These are four different questions a coach asks at different times — "how has the season gone
for this player", "who needs a decision this round", "what am I currently working on with this
player", and "which team does this player belong to" — and mixing them into one view would make
each harder to use. Keeping them separate keeps each one specific to the question it answers.

## How

- **Overview** shows played matches, goals, assists, and role counts (core, support,
  development) drawn from completed post-match reports — draft selections and finalized but
  unplayed matches are never counted as played. Selecting a player opens a preview panel with
  more detail and a link to their full profile.
- **Current round** shows the live plan-integrity state for one round: which players are
  blocked, need a decision, or are fully covered.
- **Development** lists every active player's current coaching focus, one player per row — a
  plain list, never a ranking.
- **Manage base groups** is for stable team belonging — assigning a player to their core team.
  It is separate from weekly selection: moving a player between matches for one round does not
  change their base group.

## Player profile

A player's full profile is organized into tabs, so each question has its own place:

- **Overview** — a season participation summary, recent planned opportunity, the player's
  effective position profile, the latest recorded observation, the current development focus,
  and recent matches.
- **Matches** — the football the player has actually experienced: recorded minutes and actual
  positions per match. Positions are never inferred from a planned lineup when recorded data
  exists.
- **Development** — what the coach is currently helping the player learn: the active focus, the
  chronological observation history, and completed focus areas. Never a ranking or a score.
- **Evidence** — what Matchboard knows enough to surface, grouped by Opportunity, Position,
  and Match context, each with its sample size and confidence. Where the evidence is too thin,
  it says so rather than inventing a number.
- **Manage** — the editable administrative record: player details, declared positions,
  attribute ratings, availability, coach context, readiness signals, squad/rotation context,
  and assessment history. Editing lives here rather than mixed into the read-only views.

The identity header (shirt mark, name, team, effective positions, availability) stays visible
across all tabs, and the tabs are backed by the URL — so bookmarking, sharing, and browser
Back/Forward all work as expected.

## Outfield role suitability

A player's profile page shows more than their declared primary, secondary, and tertiary
position — it shows a factual read of how well-supported each outfield role actually is for
them, using both declared position and what has actually been recorded on the pitch.

Declared position is a strong starting point, not a rigid queue. A role appears as **Natural**
when it matches the declared primary position, **Plausible** when it matches a declared
secondary or tertiary position, **Developmental** when there is no declared fit but enough
recorded match time in that role to say something meaningful, and **Unsupported** when neither
applies. Unsupported is not a judgement — it simply means there is nothing yet to base a role
change on.

Consider a squad with five players all declared primarily as Striker. If the coach needed to
share game time across a match, rotating all five through a single Striker slot while everyone
else stays on the whole match would not be a sensible answer. Some of those five may have a
declared secondary position at Winger, or may already have recorded minutes at Central Midfield
from an earlier match — those are genuinely supported alternate roles. A Striker with no
declared alternate position and no recorded time at Centre Back does not suddenly become a
plausible defender just because the team needs the minutes spread out; that role stays
Unsupported for them.

Where enough is known about a player's attributes, the profile also shows fit for a small set of
tactical functions — first-line press, pace in behind, hold-up and link play, central defensive
continuity, ball progression, and width — kept separate from position. Two players who share the
same declared position can offer different functions: one Striker might be a strong fit for
pressing the opponent's build-up, while another offers more in behind the defensive line.

This flexibility applies to outfield roles only. Goalkeeper is never inferred this way — playing
in goal takes a specific kind of readiness a coach decides on directly, not something Matchboard
works out from a player's other attributes or from a gap that happens to need filling. See
[Role suitability and position experience](/docs/how-matchboard-works/role-suitability-and-position-experience)
for the full reasoning, including how this feeds into generated lineups and rotation plans.

to see who has been sent to help other teams most often this season, and checks whether that
load has been shared fairly across the available players.

## What Matchboard does next

Season statistics never drive selection or fairness scoring automatically — they are shown for
the coach to review, not an automatic verdict. See
[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) for how observations recorded during and
after matches build into more useful context over time.
