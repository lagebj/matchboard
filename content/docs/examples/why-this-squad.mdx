title: "Worked example: Why this squad?"
description: A real generated round from Fjordvik FK, showing why two players ended up as support rather than core.
---

This example uses Fjordvik FK's real seeded data and the real squad selection engine — see
[Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine) for how the mechanism
itself works before reading this walkthrough.

## Initial conditions

Fjordvik FK fields three teams from one player pool. Going into this round:

- **Fjordvik Blå** has 10 players on its core roster, but a target squad size of 9.
- **Fjordvik Hvit** has 8 players on its core roster, also with a target squad size of 9 — one
  short of its own target before any movement happens.
- **Fjordvik Rød** has 8 players on its core roster as well.
- An active support rotation path runs from Fjordvik Blå to Fjordvik Hvit.
- All three teams have matches in the same round, generated together in one pass.

## What Matchboard generated

Fjordvik Blå's own match squad ends up with 8 players from its 10-player roster — near its
target, not the full roster. Two Blå players, Noah Mensah and Oliver Strand, are instead selected
as support for Fjordvik Hvit's match, each carrying the explanation "Selected as support for
Fjordvik Hvit after being dropped as surplus core from Fjordvik Blå." Fjordvik Hvit's squad ends
up with 10 players — 8 of its own core players plus the two support players — which is one above
its own target of 9.

## Why

- Fjordvik Hvit genuinely needed help to reach a reasonable squad: its own roster alone falls
  short of target.
- Fjordvik Blå had more core players than its own match needed to hit target, so treating all 10
  as required for its own match would have left two players without a clear purpose while a
  teammate club genuinely needed them.
- The active Blå → Hvit support rotation path is exactly what makes this movement valid — without
  it, no amount of Hvit's need could pull a Blå player into Hvit's squad automatically.
- Generating the whole round together, rather than one match at a time, is what let Matchboard see
  both facts — Blå's spare capacity and Hvit's shortfall — in the same pass and resolve them
  against each other.

## What would change the outcome

If Fjordvik Blå's own match needed all 10 players (a larger target size, or more players marked
unavailable elsewhere), fewer or none would be available to move. If the Blå → Hvit support path
were inactive, or either player were marked non-rotatable, this specific movement could not
happen automatically, and Hvit's shortfall would instead show as a plan-integrity condition for
the coach to resolve — see [Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine#squad-repair)
for what happens next in that case.

## What this does not prove

Noah Mensah and Oliver Strand are not "extra" or less valued at Fjordvik Blå. Nothing about being
sent as support reflects on their ability or their standing on their own team — it reflects that,
in this specific round, their own team's target was already covered and a teammate club had a
genuine, real need an active path could meet. The same two players could just as easily be core
for Blå in a different round if Blå's own numbers were tighter.

## What still needs a decision

Generating this round also surfaced something separate: one Fjordvik Blå player is currently
marked Unavailable but still appears in Blå's generated squad. Because availability is checked
live against the current plan, this shows as a Blocked condition — "selected but unavailable" —
until the coach either removes that player or corrects their availability. This is unrelated to
the support movement above; it is a reminder that a generated squad is a starting point, not a
final answer, and that Matchboard actively flags this kind of condition rather than leaving it
for the coach to spot by eye.
