This example uses a real planned rotation from Fjordvik FK's documentation dataset. See
[Rotation engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/rotation-engine) for the full mechanism before
reading this walkthrough. It shows a single planned change end to end — the plan, its reactive
context, and what actually happened — rather than a full multi-batch whole-match plan; a richer
example with several batches of changes across a match is a natural next addition to this
documentation.

## Initial conditions

Fjordvik Rød's match against Bergstad IF has a starting lineup with Elias Storm centrally and
Theo Falk on the wing. Before kickoff, the coach plans one change: swap their positions around
the 30-minute mark, once Bergstad IF have settled into their own shape.

## What the plan shows before kickoff

![Rotations tab showing a planned position swap at around 30 minutes, with an evidence panel showing what has tended to happen at that point in the match across three recorded matches](/docs/screenshots/planning/planned-rotations.png)

Building this change into the plan surfaces relevant context automatically: across three
recorded matches, Fjordvik Rød has scored twice and conceded nothing in this phase of the match —
shown with exactly how many matches that pattern is based on, at emerging (not yet established)
confidence. This is descriptive context attached to the moment in the plan, not a prediction that
the same thing will happen again.

## What actually happened

The coach applied the change at minute 33 — three minutes later than planned. The actual record
reflects that honestly: the plan still shows the change was planned for around 30 minutes, marked
applied, next to the real time it happened. Neither number was adjusted to match the other.

## Why

- The reactive context shown here comes from the club's own recorded match-phase history, not
  from this specific plan or this specific opponent.
- Applying later than planned is normal — matches do not run to a schedule, and Matchboard does
  not treat a delay as an error to correct.
- The plan and the actual record are shown together but kept structurally distinct: the plan says
  what was intended, the actual record says what happened.

## What would change the outcome

Moving the same change five minutes earlier or later could surface a different recorded pattern —
an earlier moment in the match may have its own history, distinct from the closing stages. A
delayed or skipped change instead of an applied one would show that outcome plainly, not be
silently dropped from the plan.

## What this does not prove

The recorded pattern behind this plan does not predict that Fjordvik Rød will score in this
window against Bergstad IF specifically, or that swapping these two players causes anything. It
is a description of what has tended to happen, based on three matches — a real but limited
sample, shown as exactly that.
