title: Evidence and learning
description: What Matchboard remembers from completed matches, and what it does and does not claim to know.
---

## What

Once a match is reported, Matchboard builds a factual history from it: minutes played by
position, who was selected alongside whom, football observations recorded by the coach, and how
matches against a given opponent have gone over time. Later views draw on this history to give
planning and reflection more context than any single match could.

## Why

A single match tells you what happened once. A season of matches recorded consistently tells
you what tends to happen — which is what makes later planning and reflection genuinely more
informed, not just a bigger pile of records. See
[Working with Matchboard](/docs/core-concepts/working-with-matchboard) for the fuller version of
this idea.

## How

- **Minutes and positions.** Actual playing time by position comes from what was recorded
  during the match — starting lineup, substitutions, and any position changes — not from the
  original plan.
- **Player combinations.** When two or more players have shared the pitch enough for a
  meaningful pattern to exist, Matchboard can show that as descriptive context — how much time
  together, in what shape — never as a compatibility score.
- **Match-phase patterns.** Matchboard can describe how a team's matches tend to unfold within
  a period — the opening minutes, right after a restart, or the closing stretch — as a factual
  record of goals scored and conceded in that window, always alongside how many matches the
  pattern is based on. Across eight recorded matches, a team might have conceded seven goals in
  the opening ten minutes: the pattern has enough evidence to be worth noticing, but Matchboard
  never claims an early goal will happen in the next match.
- **Opponent history.** Repeated meetings with the same opponent build a factual encounter
  history: results, and any recorded match-environment notes. One result against an opponent is
  not treated as a verdict on that opponent.
- **Opponent tactical tendencies.** Separate from an opponent's estimated sporting level,
  Matchboard can describe a repeated tactical tendency (for example, a high defensive line, or a
  patient build-up from the back) once a coach has recorded it as an observation enough times.
  One observation is never enough to describe a tendency — Matchboard does not infer a
  team's playing style from a result alone, only from what a coach actually observed and
  recorded, and it is explicit about how recent and how frequent those observations are.
- **Football observations.** The coach's recorded observations (see [Post-match](/docs/post-match))
  become part of a player's history over time, always in observable-behaviour language.
- **How far back evidence reaches.** Team-level patterns — how a team's matches tend to unfold,
  what a team's own combination or rotation history looks like — are scoped to one League season.
  A squad can change meaningfully between seasons, so a spring season's team pattern is not
  carried forward as if the autumn squad were the same team. Evidence about a specific player,
  by contrast, can span seasons — their recorded minutes, positions, and combinations follow them
  as they move between teams within the same group, since that history is genuinely about them,
  not about whichever team roster happened to exist at the time.
- **What can change automatically.** Some of the context above updates automatically as new
  matches are reported — for example, an opponent's encounter history growing by one entry.
  Automatic updates are always visible with an explanation of what changed and why; nothing
  updates silently.
- **What is historical.** A finalized round and a completed post-match report do not change
  after the fact. If something needs correcting — an attendance mistake, a missed
  observation — it goes through an explicit correction step, not a silent edit, and the original
  record is not lost.

## Example

By the fourth meeting between Fjordvik Rød and Bergstad IF, the opponent history page shows a
pattern across those matches, not just the most recent scoreline. Combined with recorded
observations of how Fjordvik Rød's shape held up in each of those matches, the coach has more to
plan the next meeting around than the last result alone would give them.

## What Matchboard does not claim

Evidence built up this way is decision support, not an objective measurement of a player or a
guaranteed prediction about an opponent. A handful of matches is a small, uncertain sample —
Matchboard is explicit about how much history backs a piece of evidence, and treats an unknown
or limited history as neutral, never as a mark against a player. Nothing here is presented as a
hidden score, a ranking, or a verdict a coach should defer to instead of their own judgement.

**Evidence does not create a hidden best-players system.** A weaker recorded outcome for a
developing player is never, on its own, a reason to plan them out of a match — fair opportunity
always comes first, and evidence can only ever influence how, where, or when a fair opportunity
is arranged, never whether it exists. A player or combination with little or no recorded history
is treated as unknown, not as a risk to avoid, so a squad is never quietly steered toward only
the pairings Matchboard already has evidence for.
