## What

The post-match report records the final result, confirms who actually played, and is where you
record football observations about individual players. Completing it locks the match's actual
record.

## Why

The match plan and the live record capture what was intended and what happened during play. The
post-match report is where the coach adds the read on it afterwards — an observation about a
player's decision-making, effort, or teamwell after the details are still fresh, before they
fade. See [Working with Matchboard](/docs/core-concepts/working-with-matchboard) for why this
step matters beyond the single match.

## How

If a match was live-reported, opening "After match" seeds the report directly from what was
recorded — attendance, goals, assists, and rotations do not need to be re-entered. If a match
was not live-reported, the report opens from the captured squad plan instead — the plan as it
stood when kickoff passed — and attendance is confirmed by hand.

Football observations use a fixed set of observable-behaviour codes — for example, helped a
teammate after losing the ball, or recovered position quickly — never a numeric grade or a
character judgement. A player who did not play as planned can be marked absent for that match
specifically, without affecting their team assignment for future rounds.

Completing the report locks it. Corrections after that go through the same reporting screen
rather than silently editing history.

## Example

After Fjordvik Rød's match against Bergstad IF, the coach confirms the final score, notes that
one planned starter was replaced by a late injury substitute, and records an observation that
Elias Storm recovered possession quickly after a mistake in the second half.

## What Matchboard does next

Completed reports are what [Players](/docs/players) counts as played, and what
[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) draws on to build a comparable history —
draft or unplayed matches never contribute to either.
