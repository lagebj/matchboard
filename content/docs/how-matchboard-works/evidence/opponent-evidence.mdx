---
title: Opponent evidence
description: The three distinct kinds of evidence Matchboard keeps about a specific opponent, and what a single result cannot establish.
---

Matchboard keeps three separate, deliberately distinct kinds of evidence about a recurring
opponent. They are kept apart on purpose: a strong opponent is not automatically assumed to play
a particular style, and a tactical tendency is never inferred from a scoreline alone.

## Estimated sporting level

**What creates it.** How strong an opponent has looked, based on results and the squad you
fielded against them, aggregated across every recorded meeting.

**How much is needed.** This uses its own confidence scale — unknown, low, medium, or high — built
from how many recorded meetings back the estimate. This is a genuinely different scale from the
one used for tactical tendencies and match-phase patterns below; do not read "medium" here as the
same thing as "emerging" elsewhere in this documentation.

**What it says.** A contextual read on how a fixture against this opponent tends to compare,
never a fixed rating attached to the opponent team as an entity.

## Tactical tendencies

**What creates it.** A repeated, observable style — for example, a high defensive line, or a
patient build-up from the back — recorded as a coach's own encounter observation after a match,
never inferred from the result on its own.

**How much is needed.** A single observation shows as limited evidence, not a confident tendency.
It takes several matching observations, recorded reasonably recently, before Matchboard describes
something as a real tendency rather than one coach's read of one match.

**What it says.** A description of what has actually been observed and recorded, with how recent
and how frequent those observations are always shown alongside it. Because tactical tendency and
estimated sporting level come from genuinely different evidence, a strong opponent is never
assumed to play any particular way, and a tendency is never inferred purely from a scoreline.

**Our response to a tendency.** Where a real tendency exists, Matchboard can also show the
factual results and goals-for/against across the matches behind it — purely descriptive, not a
causal claim that responding to the tendency caused those results.

## Combination evidence against this opponent

**What creates it.** The same [combination evidence](/docs/how-matchboard-works/evidence/evidence-sources)
covered elsewhere in this documentation, scoped specifically to matches against one opponent —
which of your own players have played together against this team, and for how long.

**What it says.** Factual context for planning the next meeting, not a claim about what will work
against this opponent specifically.

## What a single result cannot establish

One match against an opponent is one data point. A single loss does not make an opponent strong,
and a single win does not make them weak. Matchboard is explicit about how many recorded meetings
back any pattern it shows, and treats a new opponent — or an opponent with only one or two
recorded meetings — as genuinely unknown, not as a risk to plan around.

## Example

By the fourth meeting between Fjordvik Rød and Bergstad IF, the opponent detail page shows a
pattern across those matches rather than just the most recent scoreline — with the sporting-level
estimate, any recorded tactical tendency, and combination evidence against them shown as three
separate, clearly labelled sections rather than one blended judgement.
