---
title: How evidence enters planning
description: Exactly where each kind of evidence is actually used in generation today, and where it is deliberately shown but not yet used as a scoring input.
---

Evidence being computed and evidence actually changing a generated plan are two different facts.
This page is specific about which is true for each evidence type and each generation surface,
rather than implying every kind of evidence influences every decision.

## Squad selection

When [squad selection](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine) ranks candidates for
support or development movement, combination evidence can add a small, intent-scaled preference —
after eligibility, fairness, and position fit, never before them. No other evidence type
influences this step today.

## Suggesting a starting lineup and rotation plan together

Generating a starting lineup and rotation plan together (see
[Lineup engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/lineup-engine) and
[Rotation engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/rotation-engine)) is the one place all four
evidence-informed contributions are blended, loaded once and shared between both stages:

- fairness under-share for this match;
- a discovered role-suitability fit beyond a player's declared position;
- a capped bonus when the club has an established opponent tactical tendency that favours a
  specific tactical function;
- a capped bonus from position-context evidence.

## Generating a rotation plan on its own

When a rotation plan is generated without also generating the starting lineup, bench-candidate
scoring uses role suitability, fairness under-share, the same opponent-tendency bonus, and
position-context evidence — but not combination evidence. Combination evidence is currently wired
into starting-lineup selection and squad-level support/development ranking, not into standalone
rotation bench selection.

## Shown, but not yet a scoring input

Match-phase pattern evidence and transition-structure evidence (how a batch of changes tends to
correlate with what happens shortly after) are both real, computed evidence — visible as context
while building a rotation plan, and as part of a bench candidate's explanation — but neither
currently changes which candidate a generation pass actually chooses. This is a disclosed,
deliberate scope boundary, not an oversight: showing evidence is a smaller, safer step than
using it to decide, and Matchboard has kept the two separate here.

## Why this precision matters

Overstating where evidence is used would make a coach trust a signal that is not actually
influencing what they are looking at, or distrust a generated plan for a reason that had nothing
to do with how it was actually built. This page exists so the answer to "did evidence affect
this?" is always specific to the surface being asked about, not a blanket yes or no.
