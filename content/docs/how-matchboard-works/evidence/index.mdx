title: What evidence means
description: The full loop from a completed match to a later planning decision, and the vocabulary this documentation uses precisely.
---

[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) introduces what Matchboard remembers from
completed matches. This section goes deeper: exactly what each kind of evidence is built from,
how much of it is needed before it means anything, and precisely what it can and cannot influence.

## The loop

```text
plan
  ↓
play the match
  ↓
record/reconstruct the actual match (see Match state and the actual timeline)
  ↓
complete the post-match report
  ↓
derive evidence from the actual record
  ↓
accumulate history across matches
  ↓
use bounded evidence in later planning
```

Nothing in this loop rewrites an earlier step. A completed report is locked; the actual timeline
it produced is the one thing every evidence type below is derived from. Evidence never reaches
back and changes a historical plan or a locked report — it only ever informs a plan that has not
happened yet.

## Four different things, kept distinct

Evidence pages in this documentation are careful to separate four different kinds of claim,
because collapsing them into one sentence is where overstatement creeps in:

**Fact** — a player played 24 minutes at central midfield in a specific match. This is simply what
was recorded; there is nothing to weigh here.

**Observed pattern** — across several recorded periods, a team's matches have tended to go a
particular way while a player occupied a role, or while two players shared the pitch. This
describes what has actually happened, repeatedly, in the data.

**Uncertainty** — the pattern does not prove the player, the pairing, or the role caused the
outcome. A small number of matches is a small, uncertain sample, and every evidence page in this
documentation says explicitly how much history backs a given pattern.

**Planning use** — a pattern strong enough to matter can become one small, bounded, additive
signal among many the next plan considers — never an instruction, and never something that can
create an opportunity, override fairness, or exclude an eligible player on its own.

## Where each evidence type comes from

```text
Actual match timeline
        │
        ├── Position-context evidence   (one player, one position, vs. team's other time there)
        │
        ├── Combination evidence        (shared time between two or more players)
        │
        ├── Match-phase evidence        (how matches tend to unfold in a phase, team-wide)
        │
        └── Opponent evidence           (sporting level, tactical tendency, combination —
                                          all scoped to one recurring opponent)
                │
                ▼
   Bounded, capped, confidence-gated planning signals
   (see Confidence and guardrails, and How evidence enters planning)
```
