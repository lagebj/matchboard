---
title: Fair playing opportunity
description: What "fair" means when Matchboard plans a round, and what it deliberately does not mean.
---

## What

A match round — one or more matches sharing the same week — is the unit Matchboard plans within.
The season, or more precisely the league season, is the wider context Matchboard uses to judge
whether opportunity has been shared reasonably over time. Fairness is a scoring preference that
shapes which reasonable option Matchboard reaches for, not a hard rule that can override
eligibility or a team's required support.

## The planning unit versus the fairness horizon

A round is planned as a whole: every match in it is generated together, so a player moving from
one team to help another is resolved consistently rather than match by match. The league season
is the longer window Matchboard looks back across when judging load — how often a player has
been core, sent as support, moved for development, or missed an opportunity while available.

## One planned opportunity per player per round

A player belongs to at most one planned match squad per round. Moving a player between matches
transfers that one assignment; it never duplicates it. This is enforced server-side on every
draft-generation, manual-add, manual-move, and role-change action — not only in the interface.
Actual double-appearances that happen for real-world reasons (an emergency backfill, for example)
are recorded separately as post-match reality; they never mutate the finalized planned squad. See
[Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine) and
[Post-match](/docs/post-match).

## Availability is a hard input

Only players marked Available are automatically considered without qualification. A Tentative
player is included, with a warning to confirm before relying on them. A player marked Injured,
Sick, Away, or with Unknown availability is automatically excluded from generation — Unknown
specifically because an unconfirmed player cannot safely count toward a team's required support.
None of this can be changed by a scoring preference; only a manual override with a stated reason
can include an otherwise-excluded player.

A player marked Unavailable who still ends up in a generated squad is caught by a separate,
always-live check: plan integrity recomputes every selection's current availability on every
read, and flags a selected-but-unavailable player as a Blocked condition that needs the coach to
remove them or correct their availability before the plan is in good shape. Availability is
whatever the coach most recently set for that player — there is no separate per-round history to
track it against, so correcting a mistaken status takes effect immediately for any round not yet
past its planning boundary.

## Movement and support constraints are hard, not preferences

A player may only be selected outside their own core team when an active, correctly-directed
movement path exists for the exact role being used — support movement needs a support path,
development movement needs a development path, and so on. No amount of fairness scoring can make
an invalid path valid. A player marked non-rotatable is never used for generic squad repair,
regardless of how much a team needs the help. These are hard eligibility rules; fairness only
decides between the players who already pass them.

## Team support comes before optional movement, and before fairness

If a team needs required support to reach a viable squad, filling that need is attempted before
optional development movement, before fairness-driven rotation, and before any cosmetic balancing.
If the required support genuinely cannot be filled, Matchboard raises a plan-integrity signal
instead of silently leaving the team short — see the plan-integrity section of
[Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine).

Within that order, a player who has been sent as support in consecutive rounds is deprioritised
for a further support assignment, growing with each additional consecutive round — this rotates
who carries support load over a season instead of leaning on the same available players
repeatedly. It is a ranking preference: it never blocks a support assignment outright when no
better candidate exists, since a resolved-but-imperfect squad is better than an unfilled one.

## The full precedence order

Putting every constraint covered on this page and elsewhere in this section together, the order
Matchboard checks things in — verified against the same precedence contract the evidence-informed
scoring layer is required to follow — is:

```text
Eligibility and hard domain rules
  (availability, movement-path authorisation, non-rotatable exclusions)
        ↓
Fairness and required-support obligations
        ↓
Position and formation viability
        ↓
Structural balance and role suitability
        ↓
Bounded, capped evidence-informed signals
        ↓
Opponent and other contextual preferences
```

Nothing below a given step can override anything above it. A bounded evidence signal, for
example, can only ever choose between options that already passed every eligibility, fairness,
and structural check above it — never bypass one of them.

## Target, minimum, and maximum squad size

Target squad size is a planning aim, not a hard cap — a team can be selected above target, up to
its maximum. Minimum accepted squad size is a hard floor: going below it requires a manual
override. Maximum squad size is a hard ceiling: going above it also requires an override. A squad
that lands below target but above minimum is shown as a planning note, not a blocked condition —
useful context, not something that stops the plan from being finalisable.

## What fairness does not mean

Matchboard does not aim for equal minutes, an identical role rotation, or artificially flattened
squads. The goal is a useful squad each round, not manufactured equality — a stronger player can
still be used for support without being permanently redefined by it, and a developing player can
still receive a genuine challenge where effort and readiness support it. Fairness is one input
among several, always subordinate to eligibility, availability, and a team's required support.

## Manual changes and integrity

A coach can always adjust a generated squad by hand. Every domain rule above can be overridden
manually with a stated reason — same-round conflicts, movement-path restrictions, availability,
squad-size limits — except the two hard, non-overridable boundaries: the match or round's planning
boundary has already closed, or the player, match, or selection genuinely does not exist. Manual
changes recalculate plan-integrity signals, fairness impact, and the movement record immediately,
so the board never shows a stale picture after an edit.

## Example

Fjordvik Blå is short one player for its match against Havørn FK — a Blocked condition on the
Round Board, because the squad is below its minimum accepted size. **Repair options** on a player
chip shows a ranked list of eligible replacements drawn only from teams with an active support
path to Fjordvik Blå — not simply the strongest available players league-wide — and applying one
resolves the shortfall and clears the Blocked condition.
