---
title: Design principles
description: The stable rules that every Matchboard engine follows, and that every other deep page in this section assumes.
---

These principles hold across every planning surface in Matchboard — squad selection, lineups,
rotations, and the evidence that informs later planning. If a specific engine page and this page
ever seem to disagree, the specific page is describing an implementation detail; these
principles describe the intent that detail has to satisfy.

## Matchboard is decision support, not automatic authority

Every generated squad, lineup, and rotation plan is a proposal the coach reviews, not an
instruction that executes itself. Draft selections stay editable, a generated lineup is a
starting point you can reassign slot by slot, and a generated rotation plan is a sequence you
can edit, reorder, or remove change by change. Nothing Matchboard proposes requires the coach's
agreement to exist, but nothing it proposes takes effect on the pitch without the coach choosing
to run with it.

## The plan becomes history because of what actually happened, not because a coach clicked "Finalize"

A round or a match becomes a historical record automatically, the moment its real-world planning
boundary closes — a match's scheduled kickoff passing, or live reporting starting for it,
whichever happens first. There is no separate lock or confirm step. See
[Planning lifecycle](/docs/how-matchboard-works/planning-lifecycle) for the full detail,
including the narrow, explicit way a genuine reschedule can reopen planning.

## Fair opportunity comes before optimisation

Whether a player gets a meaningful opportunity this round takes priority over building the
strongest-looking squad or lineup. A team's required support need is resolved before optional
development movement, and before any scoring preference. See
[Fair playing opportunity](/docs/how-matchboard-works/fair-playing-opportunity) for what this
means in practice, and [Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine)
for how it plays out in one round.

## Historical evidence is bounded context, not an authority

Once matches are completed, Matchboard builds up evidence — recorded minutes by position, player
combinations that have shared the pitch, opponent history, and more. That evidence can help
choose between two otherwise reasonable options. It cannot create an opportunity out of nothing,
bypass an eligibility rule, or override fairness. Every evidence-informed adjustment in
Matchboard is deliberately capped, and a structural check on the underlying scoring runs after
every evidence-informed step to confirm that evidence never removed a candidate who was
otherwise eligible — it can reorder or add a small preference, never exclude.

## What was planned and what actually happened are two different, both-true records

A plan says what was intended. The actual match record says what really happened — who started,
who came on, who moved position, and when. Matchboard keeps these separate on purpose: recording
that a planned change happened three minutes later than intended, or that a substitution
happened that no plan anticipated, is more useful than quietly rewriting the plan to match
reality. Evidence is always built from the actual record, never from the original plan. A
dedicated "match state and the actual timeline" page in this section covers the mechanism in
full; see [Rotations](/docs/rotations) for how this shows up during a match in the meantime.

## No hidden player ranking

Matchboard does not compute or expose an overall player score, a "best XI," or a ranking of
players against each other. A generated lineup or rotation plan is built from position and role
fit, fairness, and bounded evidence — never from sorting players by a single number and taking
the top names. Two players can be equally reasonable choices for the same role at the same
moment, and Matchboard says so rather than pretending one was objectively better.

## Missing evidence is neutral, not a mark against a player

A player with little or no recorded history at a position, or with little shared time alongside
a particular teammate, is treated as unknown — not as a risk to avoid. A new player, a new
position, or a new opponent starts on equal footing with everything Matchboard has more history
about. [Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) covers how this plays out for each
specific kind of evidence, with a dedicated confidence-and-unknowns page planned for this section.

## Coach judgement stays authoritative, and overrides are accountable

A coach can override almost any rule manually — a same-round conflict, a rotation-path
restriction, availability, squad-size limits — by providing a reason. The override is recorded
and shown alongside the selection, not hidden. The only things a coach cannot override through a
normal draft edit are data-integrity boundaries: a match or round whose planning boundary has
already closed, or a player, match, or selection that does not exist.

## Evidence describes correlation, not causation

When Matchboard says a team's matches have tended to go a particular way while a player occupied
a role, or that an opponent has repeatedly shown a tactical tendency, that is a description of
what has been recorded — not a claim that the player or the tendency caused the outcome. A
handful of matches is a small, uncertain sample, and Matchboard is explicit about how much
history backs any pattern it shows.
