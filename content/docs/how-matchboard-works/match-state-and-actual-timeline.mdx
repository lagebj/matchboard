title: Match state and the actual timeline
description: How Matchboard reconstructs who was really on the pitch, and why the plan is never quietly rewritten to match it.

## What

The actual timeline is Matchboard's canonical record of what really happened on the pitch during
a match — who started, every substitution, every position change, and when each happened. It is
built from what was recorded, never from the original plan.

## The plan remains the plan. The actual timeline records what really happened.

This is the central idea behind everything on this page. A planned lineup and a planned rotation
describe intent. The actual timeline describes reality. Matchboard keeps them as two distinct,
both-true records rather than collapsing one into the other — a plan applied three minutes later
than intended stays recorded as "planned for minute 30, applied at minute 33," not silently
adjusted to say it always meant minute 33. See
[Design principles](/docs/how-matchboard-works/design-principles) for why this separation is a
stable rule across the whole product, not specific to matchday.

## How the timeline is built

The starting lineup is the timeline's first state. From there, every recorded substitution and
every recorded position change — during live reporting, or entered directly if a match was not
live-reported — extends it forward. Each recorded change carries a timestamp resolved against the
match's own clock, including any half-time or period break, so events from different periods sort
correctly against each other rather than by a clock that resets to zero at each new period. The
result is a full account of on-field composition at any point in the match: who was on the pitch,
in what position, for how long.

## Live Reporting mutates it; Follow Live only reads it

During a match, **Live Reporting** is where the score, clock, goals, assists, substitutions, and
position changes actually get recorded — the one place this canonical state changes.
**Follow Live**, a read-only view a second coach can open, loads the same canonical state and
receives the same updates as they happen, but has no recording control of any kind — nothing it
does can change the match record. Both surfaces read the identical underlying state; only Live
Reporting can write to it.

Refreshing the page, or opening the match again later, reconstructs the same state from the
underlying record rather than depending on anything held only in a browser tab — the record lives
on the server, not in transient client state.

## Planned rotations meet the actual timeline

A planned change is a prompt during live reporting: apply it, delay it, or skip it against what is
actually happening. Applying it writes the real position or player change onto the actual
timeline at the real time it happened. A skipped or delayed change is recorded as exactly that —
never silently dropped, and never allowed to quietly become "as if it had happened on schedule."
See [Rotation engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/rotation-engine) for how the plan itself is
built, and [Worked example: Why this rotation?](/docs/examples/why-this-rotation) for a real
applied-later-than-planned change end to end.

## Correcting the record after the match

The post-match report is where attendance, goals, assists, and rotations are confirmed once a
match is over — seeded directly from what live reporting recorded when a match was
live-reported, entered by hand when it was not. Completing the report locks it. A correction after
that goes through the report's own explicit reopen step, never a silent edit — the actual record
stays a reliable, stable account of what happened, correctable but never quietly rewritten.

## Where the gap between plan and reality shows up

![Planned vs Actual insight showing a round where one match's planning has already closed but the match has not yet been played or reported, listing every planned player as awaiting a report rather than a real absence](/docs/screenshots/matchday/plan-versus-actual.png)

Matchboard surfaces the difference between what was planned and what actually happened as its own
view, at both the round level and the individual match level. A planned player with no report yet
shows as awaiting one, not as a false absence — [Planning lifecycle](/docs/how-matchboard-works/planning-lifecycle)
covers why a match's planning boundary closing is a separate fact from the match having actually
been played.

```text
PLAN:    starting lineup → planned change 1 → planned change 2
ACTUAL:  starting lineup → change applied late → unplanned swap → change skipped

Evidence is always derived from ACTUAL, never from PLAN.
```

## What Matchboard does with the actual record

Everything in [Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) — recorded minutes by
position, player combinations, match-phase patterns — is derived from the actual timeline, never
from what was originally planned. A plan that never happened as written contributes nothing false
to that history; only what was actually recorded does.
