---
title: Role suitability and position experience
description: The difference between a player's declared position and their actual recorded exposure, and how Matchboard uses both without permanent labels.
---

## What

Every player has a declared primary position, and optionally a secondary and tertiary position.
Separately, Matchboard also tracks where a player has actually played, from the real match record.
Role suitability combines both into a factual read of how well-supported each outfield role is for
a player — used to widen planning options sensibly, never to narrow or permanently classify a
player.

## The four tiers

- **Natural** — matches the player's declared primary position.
- **Plausible** — matches a declared secondary or tertiary position.
- **Developmental** — no declared fit, but enough recorded match time in that role to say
  something meaningful.
- **Unsupported** — neither a declared fit nor meaningful recorded time. This is not a judgement;
  it means there is nothing yet to base a role change on.

This tiering exists for a specific reason: a declared position alone is not flexible enough to
solve real squad problems sensibly. If five players are all declared primarily as Striker, sharing
game time by rotating all five through one Striker slot while everyone else plays the whole match
is not a sensible answer — some of those five likely have a genuinely supported alternate role
(a declared secondary position, or real recorded minutes elsewhere), and some genuinely do not.
Role suitability distinguishes the two rather than treating every Striker as interchangeable, or
forcing a role change onto a player nothing supports.

## Goalkeeper is never inferred this way

This flexibility applies to outfield roles only. Whether a player can play in goal is never
derived from their other attributes or from a gap that happens to need filling — it is a direct
coach decision (`goalkeeperAbility`, an explicit setting on the player's profile), separate from
everything described here.

## Tactical function fit

Where enough attribute data exists, Matchboard also shows fit for a small set of tactical
functions, kept separate from position: first-line press, pace in behind, hold-up and link play,
central defensive continuity, ball progression, and width. Two players who share the same
declared position can differ here — one Striker might suit pressing the opponent's build-up,
while another offers more running in behind the defensive line. A function shows as not
applicable when none of the roles it depends on are supported for that player, or when the
relevant attributes are not recorded at all.

## Position experience

Position experience is the underlying recorded fact this all builds on: actual minutes a player
has spent at a given position, drawn from the real match-state record, not the original plan. It
is descriptive, always shown alongside how much of it exists, and it is never described as a
verdict — a player with limited recorded time at a position is unknown there, not weak there.

## What this does not do

Role suitability never overrides eligibility, availability, or fairness. It widens the set of
positions Matchboard can reasonably consider a player for; it never narrows a player down to one
role, and it never becomes a public label attached to a player's identity.

## Example

Fjordvik Rød's Theo Falk is declared primarily as a Winger. During the connected story match
against Bergstad IF, a second-half position swap gave him real recorded minutes centrally, playing
Elias Storm's role after they exchanged positions. That single match is not, on its own, enough to
move a central role out of Unsupported for Theo Falk — Developmental status needs a run of
recorded matches in the role, the same confidence threshold Matchboard uses everywhere it judges
how much evidence backs a pattern (see
[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning)). If the same swap happened repeatedly over
future matches, a central role for Theo Falk would become Developmental — a contextual option a
future plan could consider, without changing how he is declared on his profile.
