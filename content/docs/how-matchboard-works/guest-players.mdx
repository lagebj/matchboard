title: Guest Players
description: Why Guest Players exist, where they behave like a normal participant, and where that stops on purpose.
---

## What

A Guest Player is a reusable identity for someone taking part in a single Event or league round
without becoming a permanent member of your registered player group — a friend's child, someone
from another club, anyone helping out for one occasion. Once created, the same Guest Player
identity can be reused across multiple Events or rounds rather than being recreated each time.

## Why they exist

Youth football regularly needs an extra player for one day — a cup where a squad is short, a
friendly where a neighbouring club's player fills in. Recording that participation properly
matters (it is a real appearance, in a real match), but it must never quietly become a shortcut
into your club's own long-term player records. Guest Players exist to give that participation a
real, structured place without creating that risk.

## Where a Guest Player behaves like a normal participant

Once added to an Event's participant pool and assigned to a squad, a Guest Player:

- counts toward that squad's size, exactly like a registered player;
- appears in the participant list for match planning;
- can be placed in the starting lineup and moved between positions or the bench;
- appears in event exports alongside registered players, with a "Guest" label distinguishing them
  where useful — never hidden, and never presented as if they were a registered player.

One thing a Guest Player cannot do yet: help out on another squad's match as a temporary
mid-event helper, the way a registered player can. This is a genuine, disclosed gap rather than a
deliberate boundary — a Guest Player can only be a normal member of the one squad they are
assigned to for now.

![Fjordvik FK's Development Squad roster showing Kasper Lie labelled as a Guest alongside registered players](/docs/screenshots/events/guest-player-squad.png)

For a League round specifically, a Guest Player must first be registered as a participant in that
round before being assigned to one of its matches — a deliberate extra step, since League rounds
are a different planning unit from an Event.

## Where it stops on purpose

A Guest Player never becomes a permanent member of your player group, and never builds up the
history a registered player does:

- no season statistics — played count, goals, assists;
- no development history or football observations;
- no long-term position or combination evidence;
- never counted in league fairness calculations.

This boundary is not a missing feature — it is the entire point. A registered player's evidence
and statistics describe someone your club is genuinely developing over a season. A Guest Player's
participation is real for the day it happened, but it is not evidence about someone your club is
tracking long-term, and treating it as such would quietly contaminate a registered player's
record with data about someone else entirely.

## Lifecycle

A Guest Player is created with just a name — a source label and a note are optional context. There
is no delete action; a Guest Player no longer in use is deactivated instead, preserving any
historical participation exactly as it happened rather than erasing it.

## Example

Fjordvik FK's Development Squad for the Fjord Cup is one player short of feeling complete. The
coach adds Kasper Lie as a Guest Player, sourced as "a friend of the club," and assigns him
directly to the squad. He appears in the squad roster with a subtle Guest label, counts toward
the squad total, and would appear in the event's export exactly like any other squad member — but
nothing about his participation touches Fjordvik FK's registered player statistics or development
records.
