title: Squad selection engine
description: How Matchboard turns availability, team need, and history into a round's draft squads.
---

## What

Generating a round runs one orchestration across every match in it, in a fixed phase order.
Each phase completes before the next begins, and no phase creates a second planned selection
for a player already placed in that round.

```text
1. Per-match core selection
2. Round-level required support resolution
3. Cross-match conflict resolution
4. Development routing
5. Squad repair (repairing teams weakened by support movement)
6. Post-pipeline validation and plan-integrity signal persistence
7. Policy-derived warnings (additive, never blocking)
```

Generating a whole round together — rather than one match at a time — is what lets a player
moving from one team to support another get resolved consistently: the engine sees every match's
need at once, not one team's request in isolation.

## The candidate pool

Every active player in the organisation starts as a candidate for every match; the phases above
narrow that pool down. A player is removed from consideration, with a stated reason, when any of
these apply:

- their current availability is Injured, Sick, Away, or Unknown (Unknown specifically because an
  unconfirmed player cannot safely count toward a team's required support — see
  [Fair playing opportunity](/docs/how-matchboard-works/fair-playing-opportunity));
- the coach has pinned them out of the round entirely (a "Pin out");
- no rotation path authorises the movement role being considered (support, development, or squad
  repair) from their core team to the match's team;
- selecting them here would conflict with a plan they already have elsewhere in the same round.

A player marked Tentative is still considered, with a warning attached asking the coach to
confirm before relying on them.

## Core selection

A player whose core team matches the match's team is a straightforward core candidate, limited
only by the team's maximum squad size.

## Support before anything optional

If a team needs required support to reach a viable squad, filling that need is attempted before
optional development movement and before any fairness-driven rotation — see
[Fair playing opportunity](/docs/how-matchboard-works/fair-playing-opportunity) for the full
precedence rule. Among eligible support candidates, several things shape which specific player is
chosen: whether they fit the position still needed, how much core-team and support load they have
carried already this league season, and whether they were sent as support in the immediately
preceding round or two (a small, growing deprioritisation that rotates who carries support load
over time, without ever blocking a support assignment outright when no better candidate exists).
Where the club has enough recorded shared playing-time evidence for two specific players, that
evidence can add a small additional preference toward keeping (or, depending on coaching intent,
deliberately not keeping) them together — always the smallest, last-applied factor, and never
enough to override fairness or a genuine position need.

## Development movement

Where a rotation path supports it, a player can be moved to another team specifically for
development exposure — a harder or different context than their own core team currently offers.
This is evaluated only after required support is resolved, using the same kind of ranked
candidate list.

## Squad repair

When support or development movement leaves a team short, squad repair fills the gap, in this
priority order:

1. the team's own core player, moved as support, if their own match is on a different date and
   they can genuinely play both;
2. a player from a team connected by an active development or support rotation path, where they
   are not marked non-rotatable;
3. any other player with an active support or squad-repair rotation path to the team in need,
   again excluding non-rotatable players.

A non-rotatable player is never used for squad repair, at any priority level, regardless of how
much a team needs the help. If no valid repair exists, Matchboard raises a planning note instead
of silently leaving the team short.

## Target, minimum, and maximum squad size

Covered in full in [Fair playing opportunity](/docs/how-matchboard-works/fair-playing-opportunity):
target is an aim, minimum is a hard floor, maximum is a hard ceiling, and a squad below target but
above minimum is a planning note rather than a blocked condition.

## Plan-integrity signals

After generation, Matchboard checks the whole round and raises one of three kinds of signal:

- **Blocked** — the plan is not viable as it stands: a squad below its minimum accepted size, a
  duplicate planned assignment, or a selected player whose availability contradicts the
  selection. These need a decision before the round is in good shape.
- **Decision required** — a meaningful exception that should be consciously accepted: most
  commonly, an available, eligible player with no planned match opportunity this round.
- **Planning note** — useful context that does not block anything: a squad below target but
  playable, a preferred support level not fully met, a squad-repair below its preferred target,
  or a permitted fallback position being used.

These signals are recomputed live from current state every time the round is viewed — a stale
signal from before a fix clears itself the next time the round is checked, rather than lingering.

## Explanations

Every selection carries a reason, visible by hovering (or long-pressing, on a touch device) the
player's chip on the Round Board: which rule placed them there, and whether it was a hard rule or
a scoring preference. This is the same information the engine used to decide, not a separate
summary written after the fact.

## Manual override

A coach can adjust any generated squad by hand. Every domain rule the engine applies can be
overridden manually with a stated reason — the override is recorded and shown alongside the
selection, never silently applied. The two things a manual edit cannot do are change a
match/round whose planning boundary has already closed, or reference a player, match, or
selection that does not exist.

## Example

Fjordvik Blå's core roster has more players than its match against Solsiden SK needs, while
Fjordvik Hvit's own core roster falls short of what its match against Skogheim FK needs.
Generating the round together resolves this in one pass: two players who would otherwise sit out
as surplus core capacity at Blå — Noah Mensah and Oliver Strand — are instead selected as support
for Hvit, each carrying the explanation "Selected as support for Fjordvik Hvit after being
dropped as surplus core from Fjordvik Blå." Both moves are recorded in the movement ledger, the
authoritative record of non-core movement, not only in the selection itself.
