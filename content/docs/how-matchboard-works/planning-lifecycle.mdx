---
title: Planning lifecycle
description: When a plan is still yours to change, when it becomes history, and the one narrow way to reopen it.
---

## What

A round or a match moves through one lifecycle: editable while planning is still open, then
historical once its real-world planning boundary closes. There is no separate "Finalize" action
a coach clicks — the transition happens automatically, driven by real-world facts.

## Why a boundary, not a button

Earlier versions of Matchboard had a manual finalize step. It was removed because the fact it
recorded — "this plan is now the historical record" — is already true the moment a match's
kickoff passes or live reporting begins for it, whichever happens first. A manual finalize button
duplicated that fact and could fall out of sync with it: a coach could forget to click it, or
click it too early. Matchboard now derives the same fact from what actually happened, so there is
one source of truth instead of two.

## The boundary itself

A match's planning boundary closes at whichever of these happens first:

- its scheduled kickoff time passes, or
- live match reporting starts for it.

Closing the boundary captures the plan as it stood at that moment as the historical baseline —
draft selections become finalized, and the round's own status updates once every match in it has
closed. This capture is automatic and idempotent: it happens the next time anything checks
whether the match is still editable (opening the Round Board, starting live reporting), not on a
background schedule, and checking twice never captures twice.

```text
Draft (editable)
     │
     │  kickoff passes, or live reporting starts
     ▼
Historical baseline captured
     │
     │  match is played and reported
     ▼
Completed history
```

Nothing about the plan itself needs to be "ready" for the boundary to close. A round with unresolved
plan-integrity issues still becomes historical automatically at kickoff — Matchboard does not hold
a match open indefinitely waiting for a decision. If a coach wants to consciously accept an
issue before the boundary closes, that happens through a manual edit's own override reason (see
[Squad selection engine](/docs/how-matchboard-works/squad-selection-engine)), which is captured as
part of the plan — not through a separate finalization-time prompt.

## Draft and historical are not visually interchangeable

While a round or match is still editable, the Round Board shows it as a draft: fully editable,
plan-integrity signals recalculated live on every change. Once the boundary closes, the same
match shows a read-only "Planning closed" indicator instead of an edit surface — normal draft
actions (add, remove, regenerate, clear) are refused, since the data-integrity boundary described
in [Design principles](/docs/how-matchboard-works/design-principles) has been reached.

Becoming historical is not the same fact as being played. A match whose boundary closed at
kickoff but that has not yet been reported shows a distinct "Planning closed" state, not "Done" —
completing the post-match report is a separate later step. See [Post-match](/docs/post-match) for
that step.

## Reopening planning after a genuine reschedule

If a match's kickoff time turns out to be wrong and it genuinely has not started yet, editing the
match's date/time forward into the future reopens its planning automatically as part of that same
reschedule — there is no separate "un-finalize" action. This is refused, with a stated reason,
when the match already has recorded live activity or a completed post-match report: a reschedule
corrects a scheduling mistake, it does not retroactively pretend a played match did not happen.

Reopening one match's planning does not reopen any other match in the same round that has already
closed on its own terms.

## What this is not

This is not a way to edit history. A completed post-match report is corrected through its own
explicit reopen step (see [Post-match](/docs/post-match)), not by reopening the plan. A finalized
round's captured selections and movement history remain readable in
[Reports](/docs/reports) exactly as they were captured, whether or not the match was ever
rescheduled afterward.

## Example

Fjordvik Rød's match against Bergstad IF kicks off at 18:00. At 17:59 the Round Board still shows
it as an editable draft. At 18:01, without anyone taking any action, it shows "Planning closed" —
the plan that existed one minute earlier is now the historical baseline. If the match had
actually been postponed to the following week and the coach corrects the date in Matchboard
before 18:00 on the new date, planning reopens automatically as part of saving that correction.
