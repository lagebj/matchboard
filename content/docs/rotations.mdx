---
title: Rotations
description: Planning position changes and substitutions before a match, and recording what actually happened.
---

## What

A planned rotation is an intended position change or substitution for a specific match, set up
before kickoff. During the match, each planned change can be applied, delayed, or skipped
against what actually happens.

## Why

Coaches often know before a match that a player will start in one role and move to another
partway through, or that a substitution will happen around a certain point. Recording that
intention — and then recording whether it happened as planned — is what lets
[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) distinguish planned game management from
an unplanned change forced by the match itself.

## How

Set up planned rotations on the match's Rotations tab before kickoff: a position or player
change tied to an intended time or match phase. During live reporting, each planned change
appears as a prompt to apply, delay, or skip it. Applying it records the real position/minute
change on the actual match timeline; the plan itself is never silently rewritten to match
reality after the fact.

While you are still building the plan, the Rotations tab shows what the plan actually means —
which players share the pitch after each change, and any relevant history for that specific
combination or moment in the match. Change a substitution's timing, swap who comes on, or add
another change earlier in the plan, and this updates immediately to reflect the new plan — it is
a projection of the hypothetical plan you are looking at right now, never a prediction of what
will happen, and never something that changes the club's actual match history.

## Reactive planning

Before you commit to a plan, Matchboard can show you what it implies. If two players are
planned to be on the pitch together after a change, and the club has a season's worth of
recorded minutes for that pair, that shows up next to the change. If a change falls late in the
match and the team has a recorded pattern for that part of matches — say, goals conceded in the
closing minutes — that shows up too, with how many matches the pattern is based on.

Move the same change five minutes earlier, and the picture can change with it: a pattern tied to
the closing minutes might no longer apply, while a different one tied to an earlier phase might
now be relevant instead. None of this touches the season's actual recorded history — only the
hypothetical plan you are currently looking at.

## Example

The plan for Fjordvik Rød's match has Elias Storm starting centrally and moving to a wide role
after 30 minutes. During the match, the coach applies that change at minute 33 — three minutes
later than planned, which the actual record reflects honestly rather than adjusting the plan to
fit.

## What Matchboard does next

Comparing the plan against what actually happened is available afterwards, showing any
deviation in timing or an unplanned substitution the plan did not anticipate. This comparison,
along with the actual position record, is what
[Evidence and learning](/docs/evidence-and-learning) draws on for player-combination and
position-exposure history.
