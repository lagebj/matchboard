## What

The Round Board is where you review a round's draft squads: who is selected for each match,
who has been sent as support or moved for development, and what still needs a decision.

## Why

Generated selections are a starting point, not a final answer. A coach may know something the
generation pass does not — a player is carpooling with a teammate, or should get a specific
challenge this week — and needs to see and adjust the plan with full visibility into why each
player was placed where they are.

## How

The board has one column of available players and one column per match. Dragging a player into
a match column selects them; the role — core, sent as support, or development — is worked out
automatically from their base team and any active rotation path, or you can choose it yourself
with a reason if no path exists.

Every selection has a "Why this selection" explanation: which rule placed them there, what
fairness or load impact it has, and whether it is a hard rule or a scoring preference. Plan
integrity is shown as **Blocked** conditions (worth resolving or consciously overriding before
kickoff), **Decision required** conditions (an available player has no match this round), and
**Planning notes** (useful context that never blocks anything).

A match's own Tactics tab is where you choose its formation once the squad is settled:

## Generating a starting line-up and rotation plan together

Once a formation is chosen, "Generate lineup & rotation plan" proposes a starting line-up and a
full rotation plan for the whole match in one pass, so the two never contradict each other — the
starting eleven is chosen with the same picture of fairness and evidence the rotation plan will
also use, rather than picking a strong-looking starting group first and leaving the rotation plan
to work around whatever unfairness or awkward structure that leaves behind.

The same considerations apply to both, in this order: who most needs a meaningful opportunity
this match comes first, then whether a role genuinely fits a player, then what the club's own
recorded evidence and any opponent history can add — never the other way around. This is not a
search for the strongest possible eleven; a player is never left out of the plan because a
teammate simply has a marginally higher rating.

The result is one proposal, fully editable — the coach reviews it exactly like a plan built by
hand, and the decision to keep, adjust, or discard any part of it always stays with the coach.

## Example

Fjordvik Blå is short one player for its match against Havørn FK. The Round Board shows this as
a Blocked condition. The coach uses **Repair options** on a player chip to see a ranked list of
eligible replacements from teams with an active support path to Fjordvik Blå, and applies one.

## What Matchboard does next

Manual changes recalculate plan integrity, fairness impact, and the movement ledger immediately
— the board never shows a stale picture after an edit. There is no separate "finalize" step: the
plan stays editable here right up until a match's own kickoff (or live reporting starting),
whichever comes first — at which point it becomes historical automatically. See
[Fixtures and rounds](/docs/fixtures-and-rounds) for how that boundary works.

The Round Board is fully usable on a phone for a quick check between matches, with the same
plan-integrity summary at the top:
